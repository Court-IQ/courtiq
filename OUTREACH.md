## Where to find creator candidates

**Instagram search:**
- `#aau` `#aaubasketball` `#hsbasketball` `#basketballtraining`
  `#hoopstrainer` — sort by recent posts, click into accounts with
  consistent posting + 50K+ followers
- Look at who comments on bigger accounts' posts (their friends,
  smaller accounts in the same niche, often more reachable)

**TikTok search:**
- "basketball drills" "basketball training" "AAU recruiting"
- For You will surface basketball-niche creators if you watch a few

**YouTube:**
- Search "basketball skills" "AAU breakdown" — channels in the
  50K-500K subscriber range
- Check recently-uploaded video comments — engaged community = real
  audience

**Twitter/X recruiting accounts:**
- @overtimeNBA, @PrepHoops, @PaulBiancardi follow the right people —
  look at their replies to find smaller scouting accounts

**LinkedIn / personal networks:**
- AAU coaches you've played for
- Skills trainers you've worked with
- Anyone you know who runs camps

## Red flags when evaluating a creator

- Likes:engagement ratio is bad (lots of likes, no comments = bought
  followers)
- Posts are all reposts of NBA highlights (not their own content)
- They already have a competing product (a "training app" they pitch)
- They've taken sponsored deals from every category (low signal that
  they care about anything specific)
- Audience demographic is mostly adult men aged 35+ (not your target
  customer)

## Green flags

- They make their own original content (drills, breakdowns, vlogs)
- Comments are engaged conversations, not bot spam
- Audience demo is teen/young-adult hoopers
- They mention recruiting, training, or development as a theme
- They reply to fans (good community manager = will be good at
  selling to that community)
